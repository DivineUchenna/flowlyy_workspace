# Write Opener

Write the first message to a construction director on LinkedIn. The goal of
this message is a reply, not a meeting. Everything else follows from a reply.

This structure is proven: it got a scaffolding managing director to respond
in sixty seconds. Broadcast posts to the same audience produced arguments and
no leads. Do not deviate from the shape.

## Required input

Do not write the message until you have:

1. Company name
2. At least one specific, verifiable fact about this company — a recent
   contract win, an expansion, a stated accreditation, a figure they have
   published, a post they wrote
3. Approximate size (operatives or employees)
4. The trade and the certifications that trade actually holds

If any of these are missing, say what is missing and ask for it. Never
invent a detail to fill the gap. A wrong fact in the first line destroys
the message and the relationship.

## Structure

**Line 1 — the specific detail.**
One sentence proving you looked them up. Name the thing: the tier 1 status,
the £30k training investment, the new depot, the framework win.

**Line 2 — the question.**
One question about how they currently handle a process. It must be
answerable in a sentence, and it must be about their operation, not about
your product.

That is the whole message. Nothing else.

## Rules

- **Under 80 words.** Directors read on a phone between meetings.
- **No pitch.** Do not describe what you build. Do not mention AI,
  automation, systems, or software.
- **No call to action.** No "would you be open to a chat", no calendar link,
  no "is this something you'd be interested in". A yes/no close invites no.
- **Never claim to have spoken to other directors** unless you actually have,
  and can name how many. Borrowed authority breaks the moment someone who
  does the job pushes back.
- **Never assert their problem.** Do not say "most firms struggle with X" or
  "this is costing you money". You do not know. Ask instead.
- **Never mention competitors or other tools**, favourably or otherwise.
  Knocking alternatives is a credibility risk and reads as insecure.
- **No flattery.** "Impressive work" and "love what you're doing" mark the
  message as templated.
- Plain English. No corporate register. Contractions are fine.

## Handling the reply

The reply is where most of these are lost. When a director answers, even in
two words, the correct next move is **one more question**, never a pitch.

- Short answer ("hard mate", "it's a pain") → ask them to expand. "Hard how —
  is it the chasing, or finding out too late?"
- Detailed answer → thank them, ask the single follow-up that most threatens
  your assumption.
- Answer that contradicts your assumption → accept it. Do not argue. That is
  the most valuable reply you can get.

Never respond to a short answer by generalising it away ("many directors say
the same"). That closes the thread.

## Follow-up

If no reply after 5–7 days, send one follow-up that lowers the cost of
answering — a new, smaller question plus an explicit "a two-line answer is
plenty". Not a bump. Never more than one follow-up, then leave it six weeks.

## Output format

Return the message only, ready to paste. Then, separately, list the specific
facts used and where each came from, so they can be verified before sending.

If the research notes do not contain anything specific enough to open with,
return exactly: INSUFFICIENT RESEARCH — and say what to go and find.
